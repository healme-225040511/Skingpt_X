## 1. Condition Overview
Plantar warts are benign epithelial tumors caused by **human papillomavirus (HPV)**, most commonly located on weight-bearing areas of the sole.  
- **Clinical relevance**: Must be distinguished from corns, calluses, and melanoma to avoid mismanagement.  
- **Natural history**: Usually self-limiting; however, lesions beneath metatarsal heads or the heel often become painful and alter gait.

## 2. Evidence-Based Treatment Pathway

| Stage | Recommended options | Key evidence | Common adverse effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First-line** | Cryotherapy (liquid N₂) <br> 15–40 % salicylic acid | Meta-analysis: 60–80 % clearance | Pain, blistering, post-inflammatory pigment change |
| **Second-line** | Imiquimod 5 % cream <br> CO₂ or PDL laser <br> Aggressive cryo | For recurrent/resistant warts <br> Laser allows precise depth control | Erosion, scarring, higher cost |
| **Adjunctive** | Pressure-relieving pads <br> Oral analgesics (paracetamol/NSAIDs) | Reduces discomfort & permits normal walking | — |

## 3. Emerging Therapies & Clinical Trials
1. **Intralesional Candida antigen**  
   - Mechanism: Non-specific Th-1 immune activation → HPV-infected keratinocyte lysis  
   - Data: JAAD 2023; complete clearance 68 % vs 28 % (saline)

2. **Topical antiviral agents**  
   - 5 % ribavirin gel, HPV-E6-targeted siRNA cream – phase II trials ongoing

## 4. Patient-Specific Recommendations
- **Lesion location**: Metatarsal head + heel → favour **cryo + off-loading pad** to minimise walking interruption.  
- **Pain control**: Paracetamol 500–1 000 mg 30 min pre-/post-procedure; lidocaine 5 % patch 1 h occlusive dressing before cryo.  
- **Life-style modifications**  
  - Change cotton socks daily; UV-shoe steriliser.  
  - Wear slip-proof sandals in communal showers/pools.  
- **Cautions & contraindications**  
  - Discontinue salicylic acid/imiquimod if acute contact dermatitis; switch to laser.  
  - Avoid deep cryotherapy in diabetics or patients with peripheral neuropathy (ulcer risk).
